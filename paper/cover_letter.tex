Following transfer through Elsevier's journal transfer process, we
respectfully submit our manuscript, \textbf{``NARCIS: Authenticated Neural
Coverless Image Signaling with Attack-Qualified Codebooks and Error
Correction,''} for consideration as an Original Research Article in the
\textit{Journal of Information Security and Applications} (JISA).

\clhead{Relevance to JISA.}
NARCIS is a practical multimedia-security system for transmitting protected
messages through sequences of unchanged natural images selected from a shared
index. The work connects information-security mechanisms with an empirically
validated application: AES-GCM protects payloads and session metadata,
associated data binds sender and receiver identities, keyed symbol assignment
limits public label exposure, monotonic sequence checking rejects replay, and
Reed--Solomon coding supports exact authenticated recovery after image-channel
distortions. It therefore directly addresses JISA's interests in
authentication, cryptographic protection in systems and applications, and
multimedia security.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Applied security protocol:} confidentiality, authenticity,
identity binding, replay control, framing, error correction, and cover
selection are integrated and tested in one end-to-end implementation.
\item \textbf{Operational robustness:} a codebook is accepted only when every
protected coded message can be realised by the finite, attack-qualified image
index, rather than on nominal descriptor capacity alone.
\item \textbf{Adversarial evaluation:} calibration and holdout distortions are
separated, while selection leakage is measured with statistical, ensemble,
and learned detectors.
\end{itemize}

\clhead{Technical Contribution and Evidence.}
Across five deterministic partitions each of BOSSBase and RGB Caltech-101,
all 1,575 clean, calibration, and holdout message-condition trials recovered
the authenticated plaintext. Mean symbol accuracies were 98.74\% and 98.59\%.
A matched BOSSBase ablation fell from 210/210 to 142/210 recoveries without
qualification. At a 16-symbol operating point, net rate increased from 0.184
to 1.213 bits/cover for 8- to 128-byte payloads. The paper also reports a weak
Caltech-101 selection signal (repeated CNN AUC 0.533, 95\% CI
[0.517, 0.548]) and explicitly limits its security conclusions to the tested
datasets, partitions, attacks, threat model, and detector classes.

\clhead{Positioning Within the Journal's Criteria.}
The manuscript does not propose a new cryptographic primitive or rely on
purely theoretical security arguments. It applies established cryptography
within a reproducible communication system and evaluates complete-message
security and recovery. Nor is it a routine application of an off-the-shelf
AI model to a standard security dataset: the learned descriptor is one
component of a new protocol-level design whose principal contributions are
attack-qualified finite-index feasibility, protected-message-aware operating
point selection, and end-to-end authenticated validation. The implementation,
tests, fixed seeds, experimental evidence, and manuscript sources are publicly
available.
