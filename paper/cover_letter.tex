We respectfully submit our manuscript, \textbf{``NARCIS: Authenticated Neural
Coverless Image Signaling with Attack-Qualified Codebooks and Error
Correction,''} for consideration as a Research Paper in
\textit{Computers \& Security}.

\clhead{Scope Fitness Assessment.}
NARCIS addresses secure multimedia communication through unchanged natural
images. It combines an invariant descriptor, balanced codebooks,
attack-qualified selection, finite-index feasibility, AES-GCM protection,
replay rejection, and Reed--Solomon correction. It therefore concerns
information hiding, multimedia security, active-channel robustness, and
authenticated communication.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Security:} success requires exact authenticated plaintext
recovery, rather than descriptor accuracy alone.
\item \textbf{Operation:} an alphabet is accepted only when every coded symbol
has sufficient qualified covers in the finite index.
\item \textbf{Evaluation:} calibration and holdout attacks are separated;
selection leakage is tested with statistical, residual, and CNN detectors.
\end{itemize}

\clhead{Scientific Contribution and Evidence.}
Across five deterministic partitions each of BOSSBase and RGB Caltech-101,
all 1,575 clean, calibration, and holdout message-condition trials recovered
the authenticated plaintext. Mean symbol accuracies were 98.74\% and 98.59\%.
A matched BOSSBase ablation fell from 210/210 to 142/210 recoveries without
qualification. At a 16-symbol operating point, net rate increased from 0.184
to 1.213 bits/cover for 8- to 128-byte payloads. Calibration-locked projection
selection increased the minimum stable bucket from 92 to 161 on the evaluated
BOSSBase partition. The paper also reports a weak Caltech-101 selection signal
(repeated CNN AUC 0.533, 95\% CI [0.517, 0.548]) and makes no general
undetectability claim.

\clhead{Positioning.}
Unlike work centred on nominal hash length or single-image mapping capacity,
NARCIS tests whether a complete protected message can be realised by a finite
index and recovered after declared distortions. Its position is protocol
completeness and verified operating-point selection, not universally maximal
capacity.
