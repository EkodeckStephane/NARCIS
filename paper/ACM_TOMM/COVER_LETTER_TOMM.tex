\textbf{To the Editors}\\
\textit{ACM Transactions on Multimedia Computing, Communications, and Applications (TOMM)}

\textbf{Re: Submission of ``NARCIS: Authenticated Neural Coverless Image Signaling with Attack-Qualified Codebooks and Error Correction''}

Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript \emph{NARCIS: Authenticated Neural Coverless Image Signaling with Attack-Qualified Codebooks and Error Correction} for consideration in ACM TOMM. The work studies a multimedia-security problem specific to selection-based coverless communication: reliable authenticated signaling from a finite shared image index while preserving broad index participation and measuring the statistical footprint of secret-dependent selection.

NARCIS couples a calibration-locked neural codebook, complementary five-cover group banks, signature-balanced keyed selection, exact cyclic Gray session balancing, AES-GCM authenticated framing, and Reed--Solomon correction. The final K=8/group-size-5/RS128 protocol was frozen from BOSSBase development evidence before external validation on five Caltech-101 partitions.

The completed evidence stack includes 1,800/1,800 external calibration recoveries, complete use of every 7,000-cover Caltech index, and 1,163/1,200 authenticated recoveries across eight unseen holdout transformations. Seven holdouts achieve 150/150 recovery; the strongest 12\% crop yields 113/150 and defines the observed robustness boundary. Repeated image-disjoint steganalysis produces mean macro-AUC 0.504 for SRM-lite, 0.513 for GLCM, and 0.527 for a trained 30-head CNN, supporting a low but measurable selection-leakage characterization.

The revision also includes a frozen execution of the official DiffStega benchmark. All 100 UniStega cases completed, producing 700 PNG outputs; independently reproduced correct-recovery PSNR is 23.274 dB compared with 23.290 dB in the original study. The manuscript preserves the distinct semantics of generated-image reconstruction and authenticated finite-index byte signaling by reporting each family through its native metrics.

The public NARCIS repository contains protocol freezes, canonical machine-readable results, detector outputs, commands, hashes, and DiffStega execution evidence. Recent TOMM work on generative steganography, image steganalysis, adversarial embedding, and multimedia authentication is integrated into the scientific positioning.
