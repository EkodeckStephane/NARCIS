\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Image steganography traditionally hides information by modifying a carrier, whereas coverless approaches seek to communicate through selection, indexing, semantic mapping, or generation. The absence of pixel modification removes one important source of embedding artefacts, but it does not by itself establish steganographic security: a message-dependent selection policy can change the distribution of transmitted images, and a channel perturbation can make an otherwise valid index unusable. Recent surveys likewise separate cover-selection design from detectability and stress the need for evaluation criteria that reflect the selected-carrier distribution \cite{azam2025coversurvey,kombrink2025survey}.

Selection-based coverless systems face a second practical constraint that average descriptor accuracy does not capture. A sender has a finite shared index. After robustness qualification, every protected message induces a concrete multiplicity demand over codebook symbols. If one required symbol lacks enough reliable carriers, the message is infeasible even when average classification accuracy is high. Recent stability-aware work improves robust quantization and representative selection \cite{guo2026capacity}, while restoration and deep-hashing methods target geometric robustness \cite{meng2025restoration,tan2026hashing}. These advances do not make finite-index feasibility, authenticated message recovery, and selection detectability interchangeable objectives.

NARCIS addresses this operating regime as an end-to-end multimedia signaling protocol. Its scientific object is not a new primitive such as AES-GCM, Reed--Solomon coding, Gray coding, or self-supervised representation learning in isolation. The contribution is the joint finite-index construction that (i) locks a channel-aware codebook using calibration-only evidence, (ii) partitions each label class into complementary groups whose majority failures are explicitly characterized, (iii) schedules those groups to preserve the natural calibration-failure-signature proportions, and (iv) binds the resulting signaling path to authenticated sessions and error correction. This yields a falsifiable evaluation unit: exact authenticated plaintext recovery from a fixed finite index under declared channel and detector threat models.

The final TOMM validation deliberately separates development from external evidence. BOSSBase 1.01 \cite{bas2011boss} was used to develop and freeze the operating point. Caltech-101 \cite{feifei2004caltech101,caltech101data} was then evaluated with $K=8$, five covers per coded symbol, 128 Reed--Solomon parity bytes, 30 authenticated sessions per partition, and no partition-specific repair. The external campaign adds two gates absent from earlier versions of the work: unseen channel holdouts and repeated selection-steganalysis with SRM-lite, GLCM, and CNN detectors. We also execute a frozen external DiffStega benchmark \cite{yang2024diffstega} as a reproducibility and positioning control, while keeping family-specific metrics separate.

The evidence supports four bounded conclusions. First, the calibration-locked group-bank construction is feasible across all five external partitions and recovers all 1,800 calibration messages. Second, external holdout robustness is strong but not universal: 96.92\% of 1,200 holdout messages authenticate, with all failures localized to the strongest crop. Third, selection is not empirically invisible: the strongest trained detector obtains mean macro-AUC 0.527. Fourth, the DiffStega reproduction confirms that the external baseline environment is executable and close to its published PSNR reference, but it does not create a common capacity metric between generated-image reconstruction and finite-index authenticated signaling.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Selection, retrieval, and robust coverless communication}

Natural-image coverless schemes have used block statistics, retrieval descriptors, object semantics, hashing, and learned features to map messages to carriers. Multi-object recognition \cite{luo2021multiobject} and joint selection/generation designs such as JoCS \cite{ren2024robust} illustrate the diversity of communication objects and mapping rules. Guo and Ping \cite{guo2026capacity} are particularly close to NARCIS in spirit: their stability-aware pseudo-Zernike quantization and representative selection explicitly balance robustness and nominal capacity. NARCIS instead focuses on a finite shared index whose complete protected-message demand must be satisfiable, and it evaluates success only after authenticated decryption.

Geometric perturbations are a persistent failure mode. Meng et al. \cite{meng2025restoration} restore attacked images before the underlying coverless decoder, while Tan et al. \cite{tan2026hashing} use deep unsupervised hashing to improve resistance to geometric attacks. NARCIS takes a complementary approach: calibration transformations enter codebook and group-bank construction, whereas eight stronger/intermediate transformation values remain unseen holdouts. The resulting crop failure boundary is retained in the final claims rather than absorbed by retuning.

\subsection{Generative steganography and multimedia security}

Generative approaches communicate through synthesized content rather than selection from a finite natural-image index. DiffStega reconstructs a secret image through prompt- and password-conditioned diffusion without per-task training \cite{yang2024diffstega}. Guidance-feature-distribution steganography embeds information in a generative feature distribution \cite{sun2024guidance}, and progressive generative steganography in TOMM jointly studies capacity, extraction, and detectability during high-resolution generation \cite{zhou2025pgs}. These methods operate under different capacity and fidelity semantics from NARCIS. We therefore use DiffStega as an executable recent reference but do not equate perceptual reconstruction metrics with authenticated plaintext bits per selected natural cover.

Authentication also has multiple multimedia meanings. FaceSigns \cite{neekhara2024facesigns}, for example, embeds semi-fragile authentication information into media. NARCIS instead keeps transmitted indexed covers unchanged and authenticates the protected session/payload; the two problems should not be conflated.

\subsection{Steganalysis of selection policies}

Modern steganalysis makes clear that ``unchanged image'' is not synonymous with ``undetectable communication.'' CNN-based image steganalysis can exploit subtle distributional differences \cite{li2023siairnet}; adversarial embedding work likewise shows that security conclusions depend on the steganalyst under evaluation \cite{wang2024adversarial}. Cao, Wang, and Zhang specifically analyze cover-selection steganography and show that texture statistics can expose selection bias \cite{cao2025glcm}. NARCIS therefore treats the selected-vs.-non-selected distinction as an explicit threat model and reports three detector families rather than a single favorable test.
