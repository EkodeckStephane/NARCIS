\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

NARCIS treats selection-based coverless image communication as a finite-index authenticated signaling problem. Its final protocol combines a learned invariant descriptor, calibration-locked $K=8$ quantile codebooks, complementary five-cover group banks, signature-balanced keyed selection, exact cyclic Gray cluster balancing, AES-GCM authenticated sessions, replay control, and Reed--Solomon correction. On five externally frozen Caltech-101 partitions, all 1,800 calibration messages authenticate. Across 1,200 unseen holdout trials, 1,163 authenticate; all 37 failures occur under 12\% central crop, making the robustness boundary explicit. The final detector battery places SRM-lite near chance, GLCM at mean AUC 0.513, and a trained CNN at 0.527, so the supported security statement is low but measurable selection leakage under the evaluated threat model, not undetectability. A frozen 100-case DiffStega execution provides a reproducible external reference while preserving the distinction between generated-image reconstruction and finite-index authenticated byte signaling. The remaining research problem is therefore not to erase the observed limits in wording, but to reduce strong-crop failure and learned selection leakage under broader external corpora and wardens.
