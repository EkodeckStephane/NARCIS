\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the external evidence establishes}

A single frozen protocol transfers across five Caltech partitions with fixed $K$, group size, parity, projection-ranking rule, mapping, and detector settings. Moderate Gaussian noise, blur, 8\% crop, and 9-degree rotation remain fully recoverable at the authenticated-message level. The 12\% crop condition reaches the 64-byte RS correction radius and concentrates the 37 message failures. Reed--Solomon coding therefore contributes directly to the observed robustness: crop 8\% requires as many as 34 corrected byte positions, while the strongest crop reaches the decoder limit.

The group-bank mechanism also demonstrates the value of complementary cover failures. A five-cover group can remain majority-correct when individual members fail on different transformations. This enables every final external schedule to exercise all 7,000 covers while attaining perfect calibration recovery. The result supports channel qualification at the communication-unit level---the five-cover group---and preserves broad index participation.

\subsection{Detectability as a separate security axis}

The detector hierarchy reveals three levels of evidence. SRM-lite residual statistics remain close to chance, GLCM detects a weak texture/selection shift, and the CNN identifies the strongest signal. This ordering is consistent with selection leakage acting primarily as a distributional effect rather than as a pixel-embedding artefact. The supported characterization is therefore low but measurable selection leakage under the matched-index warden.

The CNN mean AUC of 0.527 describes this specific selected-vs.-remainder discrimination problem. Sequence-level traffic features, temporal metadata, semantic profiling, and adaptive deployment-specific wardens would extend the threat model beyond the present image-content audit.

\subsection{Why the exact cyclic map matters}

Finite protected messages can produce nonuniform coded-symbol frequencies. A fixed symbol-to-cluster permutation would therefore translate message-level skew into persistent visual-cluster skew. The cyclic keyed Gray map removes that persistent assignment deterministically: over each complete $K$-session cycle, every coded symbol visits every visual cluster exactly once. This algebraic property complements the empirical signature-balanced group scheduler and reduces one avoidable source of repeated-session bias before detector evaluation.

\subsection{Development-to-external transfer}

The BOSSBase and Caltech results together provide a useful transfer picture. BOSSBase development evidence reaches 1,800/1,800 calibration recoveries across five seeds, near-complete index participation, and aggregate development AUCs close to 0.5. The externally frozen Caltech protocol then reaches the same 1,800/1,800 calibration recovery while exercising every cover in each 7,000-image index. Holdout performance remains high at 96.92\%, while the 12\% crop reveals the principal transfer boundary. This pattern supports the value of freezing protocol design before external evaluation and reporting both successful transfer and the strongest observed failure regime.

\subsection{Relation to recent coverless and generative work}

NARCIS and Guo--Ping address complementary priorities. Guo--Ping optimize robust/high-capacity mapping under their published datasets and attack protocol \cite{guo2026capacity}; NARCIS emphasizes finite protected-message feasibility, authenticated recovery, complete-index usage, and explicit detector auditing at a fixed external alphabet $K=8$. DiffStega demonstrates a generative regime in which a secret image is reconstructed with diffusion models \cite{yang2024diffstega}. The completed 100-case reproduction strengthens the empirical positioning by making the recent comparator executable under a frozen environment, while the article keeps reconstruction metrics and finite-index signaling metrics in their respective semantic domains.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{External validity.} Caltech-101 provides one external natural-image corpus, while BOSSBase supports development. Validation on newer large native-resolution corpora, social-media recompression pipelines, and heterogeneous acquisition devices would broaden the domain evidence.

\paragraph{Channel boundary.} The robustness claim covers the declared transformation families and strengths. The 12\% crop condition defines the strongest observed boundary in the present protocol. Perspective warps, larger crops, occlusion, color transformations, arbitrary resampling chains, and learned image editing provide natural directions for broader channel evaluation.

\paragraph{Warden boundary.} The SRM-lite, GLCM, and CNN battery spans residual, texture, and learned selected-vs.-remainder discrimination. Traffic-analysis features, semantic side information, temporal correlation, and adaptive deployment-specific wardens would extend this security evaluation. Repeated-split intervals summarize variability because the splits overlap.

\paragraph{Cryptographic boundary.} AES-GCM, HMAC, and Reed--Solomon coding are standard components. The contribution lies in their integration with the finite-index signaling protocol and the explicit session-balancing/feasibility construction. The public benchmark key/nonce derivation serves reproducibility; operational deployment can substitute institutionally managed keys and nonces.

\paragraph{Comparator boundary.} DiffStega executes faithfully at its frozen upstream commit. Metric extraction uses an independent environment because the public benchmark package and the paper's metric-production pipeline are separate artifacts. Two metric-only package versions differ from the preregistered versions, and the manuscript reports the resulting values as independently reproduced metrics.
