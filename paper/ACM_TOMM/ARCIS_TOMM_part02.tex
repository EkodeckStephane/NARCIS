\subsection{Finite-index feasibility}
For protected envelope $c$, let $d_k(c)$ denote the number of group occurrences required for label $k$ after coding and session mapping, and $a_k$ the number of available groups in label bank $k$. The session is feasible when
\begin{equation}
d_k(c)\le a_k \qquad \text{for every }k.
\end{equation}
Nominal alphabet capacity $\log_2 K$ gives coded bits per label, while this condition tests complete-message realizability under finite group availability and within-session unique-cover usage.

Two exact properties hold before experimental evaluation. First, for any fixed coded symbol $v$ and starting sequence $q_0$, the multiset $\{\pi_{q_0+t}(v):0\le t<K\}$ contains every cluster label exactly once. Second, under unique-cover usage and a preconstructed group bank, the finite-index inequality is necessary and sufficient for emitting the complete coded session. Necessity follows because the $d_k(c)$ occurrences mapped to label $k$ must occupy distinct groups, so $d_k(c)>a_k$ forces a collision by the pigeonhole principle. For sufficiency, when $d_k(c)\le a_k$ for every label, choose an injection from the $d_k(c)$ occurrences into the $a_k$ groups of label $k$. Label banks are disjoint and groups partition their corresponding cover buckets, so the union of these label-wise injections assigns one distinct group to every coded occurrence and yields a complete transmission without within-session cover reuse.


For planning before a concrete payload is known, the deterministic condition can be lifted to a conditional blocking-risk model. Let $m$ be the number of coded symbol groups and assume, explicitly as an engineering idealization, that encrypted coded symbols are i.i.d. uniform over the $K$ labels. Then
\begin{equation}
(D_0,\ldots,D_{K-1})\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}\!\left(m;\frac{1}{K},\ldots,\frac{1}{K}\right),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{block}}=\Pr(\exists k:D_k>a_k).
\end{equation}
For arbitrary label capacities,
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{ok}}=\frac{m!}{K^m}[z^m]\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{a_k}\frac{z^j}{j!}\right),\qquad P_{\mathrm{block}}=1-P_{\mathrm{ok}}.
\end{equation}
The multinomial formulation serves as an engineering planning model for pre-session blocking risk under the stated uniform-symbol assumption. For the balanced 7,000-image external index, each label has $a_k=175$ disjoint five-cover groups. Table~\ref{tab:blockrisk} reports the resulting blocking-risk values for the evaluated payload sizes.

\section{Experimental Design}
\subsection{Development and external evidence}
BOSSBase 1.01 provides the development corpus for protocol design and operating-point selection. The final development lineage uses the same $K=8$, group-size-5, RS128, 30-session construction later frozen for external validation. Caltech-101 supplies the external corpus, with 9,144 readable source images. For each seed in $\{11,29,47,71,101\}$, a deterministic permutation assigns 1,500 images to descriptor training and the next 7,000 images to the cover index. Training and index sets are image-disjoint within each run. Across seeds, the runs are resamplings of the same 9,144-image corpus rather than independent external replications: pairwise 7,000-image index overlap ranges from 5,320 to 5,371 images (76.0--76.7\%), and 2,374 images occur in all five indexes. The five runs therefore characterize sensitivity to train--index assignment. Caltech images are converted uniformly to RGB before the $256\times256$ channel fit.

The frozen protocol design uses $K=8$, group size five, 128 parity bytes, and 30 authenticated sessions per Caltech resampling. Ten deterministic plaintexts are generated at each of 8, 32, and 64 bytes; authenticated sequences are 0--9, 10--19, and 20--29 respectively. A public deterministic benchmark root derives resampling-specific keys and nonces for reproducibility; deployment key management remains a separate operational layer.

\subsection{Calibration and holdout channel tests}
The twelve calibration transformations are those listed above. The holdout set contains eight intermediate or stronger parameterizations from the same four attack families: Gaussian noise 9 and 15; blur 1.2 and 1.8; central crop 8\% and 12\%; and rotations 5 and 9 degrees. Each of 30 sessions is evaluated under every parameterization for each of five resamplings, yielding 1,800 calibration trials and 1,200 holdout trial outcomes. Trial success requires exact authenticated plaintext recovery, so symbol accuracy and error-correction counts remain diagnostic quantities beneath the final message-level criterion.

\subsection{Frozen RS-parity ablation and communication accounting}
The learned representation, projection-selection rule, codebook, complementary group banks, session keys, payloads, and holdout label predictions are held fixed while RS parity is evaluated at 0, 32, 64, 96, and 128 bytes. The coded payload is rescheduled at each level because FEC expansion changes the required 3-bit symbol demand. Communication cost is the number of unchanged natural images transmitted; since each coded symbol consumes a five-cover group, an $m$-group session sends $5m$ images. The same 1,200 resampling--session--attack outcomes are reused at every parity level. Because attacks are repeated within sessions and the five Caltech runs overlap substantially, the parity sweep is reported descriptively by parity, attack, and resampling seed rather than treating the 1,200 outcomes as independent Bernoulli trials. Paired gain/loss counts are retained as direct within-outcome diagnostics.

\subsection{Selection-detectability audit}
The detector audit reconstructs the complete 30-session selected-cover schedule for every Caltech resampling. For each resampling and each of 20 repetitions, a 65/35 image-disjoint train/test split is created and reused across SRM-lite residual features with ExtraTrees, GLCM texture features with logistic regression, and a 30-head CNN. AUC is macro-averaged across the 30 session heads within each split. The 20 repetitions characterize within-resampling split stability. The five resampling means are reported descriptively as sensitivity to train--index assignment; cross-seed image overlap precludes treating them as independent external replications.
