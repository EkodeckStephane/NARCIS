\subsection{RS-parity ablation and communication cost}
The controlled parity ablation shows a monotone reliability--traffic tradeoff. With no RS parity, group-majority decoding alone recovers 829/1,200 outcomes (69.08\%). Recovery rises to 1,064/1,200 (88.67\%) at RS32, 1,115/1,200 (92.92\%) at RS64, 1,145/1,200 (95.42\%) at RS96, and 1,163/1,200 (96.92\%) at RS128. In paired outcome accounting, the four adjacent transitions contain 235/0, 51/0, 30/0, and 18/0 gain/loss counts, respectively, and the RS0--RS128 endpoint contains 334 gains and zero losses. These counts are descriptive because attacks are repeated within sessions and the five Caltech runs are overlapping resamplings of one corpus. Mean transmitted images per session rise from 970 to 1,396.7, 1,825, 2,250, and 2,676.7. At RS128, 8-, 32-, and 64-byte plaintexts require 2,320, 2,640, and 3,070 transmitted images, corresponding to 0.0276, 0.0970, and 0.1668 useful bits per transmitted image. Table~\ref{tab:rsablation} reports the paired parity results, Table~\ref{tab:commcost} gives payload-specific communication cost, and Fig.~\ref{fig:rstradeoff} visualizes the resulting reliability--traffic tradeoff.
