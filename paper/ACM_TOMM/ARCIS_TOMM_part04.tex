\subsection{RS-parity ablation and communication cost}
The controlled parity ablation shows a monotone reliability--traffic tradeoff. With no RS parity, group-majority decoding alone recovers 829/1,200 trials (69.08\%, exact 95\% CI 66.38--71.69\%). Recovery rises to 1,064/1,200 (88.67\%, 86.74--90.41\%) at RS32, 1,115/1,200 (92.92\%, 91.32--94.30\%) at RS64, 1,145/1,200 (95.42\%, 94.08--96.53\%) at RS96, and 1,163/1,200 (96.92\%, 95.77--97.82\%) at RS128. In the paired trial outcomes, the four adjacent transitions contain 235/0, 51/0, 30/0, and 18/0 gain/loss discordances, respectively; every adjacent exact McNemar contrast remains significant after Holm adjustment ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}\le7.63\times10^{-6}$). The RS0--RS128 endpoint comparison contains 334 gains and zero losses ($p_{\mathrm{exact}}=5.71\times10^{-101}$). Mean transmitted images per session rise from 970 to 1,396.7, 1,825, 2,250, and 2,676.7. At RS128, 8-, 32-, and 64-byte plaintexts require 2,320, 2,640, and 3,070 transmitted images, corresponding to 0.0276, 0.0970, and 0.1668 useful bits per transmitted image.
