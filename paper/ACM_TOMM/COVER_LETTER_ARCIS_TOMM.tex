\begin{letter}{Editors\\ACM Transactions on Multimedia Computing, Communications, and Applications (TOMM)}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

We submit the research article entitled ``\textbf{ARCIS: Authenticated Robust Cover-Selection Image Signaling under Finite-Index Constraints}'' for consideration in \emph{ACM Transactions on Multimedia Computing, Communications, and Applications}.

ARCIS studies selection-based coverless image communication as a finite-index authenticated signaling problem. Its contribution couples a calibration-locked codebook, complementary five-cover group banks, exact session-balanced keyed Gray mapping, authenticated framing, replay control, Reed--Solomon correction, and an explicit feasibility test for complete protected messages. Standard cryptographic and coding primitives are used as components; the scientific contribution is the integrated finite-index construction and its frozen external evaluation.

The final operating point ($K=8$, five covers per coded symbol, RS128) was fixed on BOSSBase development evidence before external validation on five Caltech-101 partitions. External calibration recovered 1,800/1,800 authenticated messages; unseen holdouts yielded 1,163/1,200 authenticated recoveries (96.92\%), with all 37 failures concentrated in the 12\% crop. A controlled parity sweep raises recovery from 829/1,200 (69.08\%) at RS0 to 1,163/1,200 (96.92\%) at RS128, while mean transmitted images per session rise from 970 to 2,676.7. At RS128, 8-, 32-, and 64-byte payloads require 2,320, 2,640, and 3,070 images. The detector audit yields mean macro-AUC 0.504, 0.513, and 0.527 for SRM-lite, GLCM, and CNN wardens.

The manuscript explicitly limits its claims: it does not claim universal undetectability, universal robustness, or direct numerical superiority over generative steganography. It reports the 12\% crop boundary, measurable learned-selection leakage, the single external natural-image corpus, and the evaluated warden scope. It also cites recent TOMM work and closely related multimedia-security literature.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submitted version. The author order is Arthur Ulrich Ewane; Juvet Karnel Sadié; Stéphane Gaël R. Ekodeck; Serge Alain Ebélé; Vivien Loïck Beyala Kamgang; Damaris Belle M. Fotso; and Chantal Marguerite Mveh-Abia. Chantal Marguerite Mveh-Abia is the corresponding author.

We believe the work fits TOMM through its combination of multimedia representation, robust image communication, security evaluation, and reproducible empirical analysis.
