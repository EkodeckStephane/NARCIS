\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

NARCIS is being repositioned for multimedia covert communication rather than
as an isolated image-feature construction. The revision distinguishes three
complementary requirements: robust multimedia transport, message-level
protection, and empirical resistance to selection-oriented steganalysis.
Recent TOMM work demonstrates that capacity, extraction accuracy, and
anti-detectability must be evaluated jointly in generative steganography
\cite{zhou2025pgs}, while TOMM steganalysis research emphasizes robustness to
cover mismatch and heterogeneous image sources \cite{li2023siairnet}.
Distribution preservation is also an explicit concern in generative image
steganography \cite{sun2024guidance}, and adversarial embedding work shows
that security conclusions depend on the steganalyst used in evaluation
\cite{wang2024adversarial}.

The revised NARCIS manuscript will therefore report only operating points that
are simultaneously feasible for the protected payload, qualified under the
declared image channel, evaluated under multiple selection detectors, and
validated end-to-end through authenticated decryption. Its media-authentication
layer is positioned separately from semi-fragile watermarking approaches such
as FaceSigns \cite{neekhara2024facesigns}: NARCIS authenticates the protected
message and session while transmitting unchanged indexed images, whereas
semi-fragile watermarking embeds authentication information into media pixels.
