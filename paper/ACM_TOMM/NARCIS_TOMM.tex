\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

NARCIS is repositioned as an end-to-end multimedia covert-communication
protocol rather than as an isolated image-feature construction. The revision
distinguishes three complementary requirements: robust multimedia transport,
message-level protection, and empirical resistance to selection-oriented
steganalysis. Recent TOMM work demonstrates that capacity, extraction
accuracy, and anti-detectability must be evaluated jointly in generative
steganography \cite{zhou2025pgs}, while TOMM steganalysis research emphasizes
robustness to cover mismatch and heterogeneous image sources
\cite{li2023siairnet}. Distribution preservation is also an explicit concern
in generative image steganography \cite{sun2024guidance}, and adversarial
embedding work shows that security conclusions depend on the steganalyst used
in evaluation \cite{wang2024adversarial}.

The revised NARCIS manuscript will therefore report only operating points that
are simultaneously feasible for the protected payload, qualified under the
declared image channel, evaluated under multiple selection detectors, and
validated end-to-end through authenticated decryption. Its media-authentication
layer is positioned separately from semi-fragile watermarking approaches such
as FaceSigns \cite{neekhara2024facesigns}: NARCIS authenticates the protected
message and session while transmitting unchanged indexed images, whereas
semi-fragile watermarking embeds authentication information into media pixels.

\section{Positioning Against Recent Coverless Multimedia Communication}
\label{sec:positioning}

Recent coverless steganography spans fundamentally different carrier models.
DiffStega is a training-free diffusion-based framework that reconstructs a
secret image through a password-dependent reference image and prompt-guided
diffusion process \cite{yang2024diffstega}. Its operating unit is therefore a
generated image and recovered visual content. NARCIS instead communicates a
protected byte string by selecting unchanged images from a finite shared
index. The TOMM evaluation treats DiffStega as an executable recent baseline,
but compares only metrics that retain the same interpretation across these
two carrier models; visual reconstruction quality is not equated with
finite-index plaintext rate.

Closer to the NARCIS selection setting, Guo and Ping combine pseudo-Zernike
moments, stability-aware quantization, and stability-regularized representative
selection to improve both robustness and nominal capacity
\cite{guo2026capacity}. Their method is the principal conceptual comparator
for stability-aware cover selection. Because no verified official
implementation has been located, its published measurements remain labeled as
literature results under the original datasets and attacks rather than being
presented as a matched reimplementation.

Geometric robustness remains a major failure mode for retrieval and
hash-based coverless methods. Tan et al. use deep unsupervised hashing with
attention and Transformer components to strengthen recovery under geometric
attacks \cite{tan2026hashing}. Meng et al. take a complementary receiver-side
approach: classify the encountered image attack and restore the received image
before applying the underlying coverless decoder \cite{meng2025restoration}.
These works motivate two distinct TOMM evaluation requirements for NARCIS:
strong holdout geometric transformations and explicit reporting of complete
message recovery rather than nominal descriptor accuracy alone.

A separate threat comes from the selection mechanism itself. Even unchanged
covers can become distinguishable when transmission preferentially samples a
statistically atypical subset of the shared index. The revised protocol
therefore evaluates both conventional residual/global detectors and a
texture-oriented GLCM detector following the cover-selection steganalysis
framework of Cao et al. \cite{cao2025glcm}. This detector battery is used as an
evaluation instrument; detector outcomes are not used to tune a projection on
holdout data.
