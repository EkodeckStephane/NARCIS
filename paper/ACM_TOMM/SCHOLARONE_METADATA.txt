Abstract
Selection-based coverless image communication preserves carrier pixels while shifting the design challenge toward reliable symbol recovery, finite-index availability, communication cost, and the statistical properties of the selected image stream. This work introduces ARCIS (Authenticated Robust Cover-Selection Image Signaling) to provide an authenticated finite-index signaling protocol whose feasibility, robustness, coding overhead, and selection detectability can be evaluated within one reproducible framework. ARCIS combines a learned normalized image descriptor, a calibration-locked K=8 quantile codebook, complementary five-cover group banks, a keyed cyclic mapping with Gray-indexed symbol order, AES-GCM authenticated framing, replay control, Reed--Solomon (RS) coding, and explicit finite-index feasibility analysis. The operating point was established on BOSSBase 1.01 and externally evaluated through five seeded train--index resamplings of Caltech-101. Within each resampling, the 1,500-image descriptor-training subset and 7,000-image cover index are disjoint; across seeds, the runs overlap because they resample the same 9,144-image corpus. Evaluation used twelve calibration transformations, eight held-out parameterizations from the same four attack families, a five-level RS-parity ablation, finite-index blocking-risk analysis, and residual, texture-based, and CNN selection detectors. External calibration achieved 1,800/1,800 authenticated recoveries, while held-out conditions achieved 1,163/1,200 recoveries (96.92%), with all 37 failures concentrated in the 12% central-crop condition. A separate control-plane audit completed 150/150 metadata roundtrips and rejected all tested tamper, replay, and wrong-receiver cases (5/5 each). Recovery increased from 829/1,200 (69.08%) at RS0 to 1,163/1,200 at RS128, while mean transmitted images per session increased from 970 to 2,676.7. At RS128, 8-, 32-, and 64-byte payloads required 2,320, 2,640, and 3,070 images, respectively. Detector means across the five resamplings were 0.504, 0.513, and 0.527 for SRM-lite, GLCM, and CNN, with resampling ranges of 0.5014--0.5052, 0.5120--0.5138, and 0.5232--0.5301; these summaries are descriptive because cross-seed image overlap prevents treating the five runs as independent external replications. These results establish a reproducible finite-index authenticated signaling construction and quantify the principal tradeoffs among robustness, communication cost, index feasibility, and selection discrimination under the evaluated image-channel conditions.
