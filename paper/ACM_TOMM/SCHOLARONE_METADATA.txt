Abstract
Selection-based coverless image communication preserves carrier pixels while shifting the design challenge toward reliable symbol recovery, finite-index availability, communication cost, and the statistical properties of the selected image stream. This work introduces ARCIS (Authenticated Robust Cover-Selection Image Signaling) to provide an authenticated finite-index signaling protocol whose feasibility, robustness, coding overhead, and selection detectability can be evaluated within one reproducible framework. ARCIS combines a learned normalized image descriptor, a calibration-locked K=8 quantile codebook, complementary five-cover group banks, a keyed cyclic mapping with Gray-indexed symbol order, AES-GCM authenticated framing, replay control, Reed--Solomon (RS) coding, and explicit finite-index feasibility analysis. The operating point was established on BOSSBase 1.01 and externally evaluated on five image-disjoint Caltech-101 partitions using twelve calibration transformations, eight unseen holdout transformations, a five-level RS-parity ablation, finite-index blocking-risk analysis, and residual, texture-based, and CNN selection detectors. External calibration achieved 1,800/1,800 authenticated recoveries, while unseen holdouts achieved 1,163/1,200 recoveries (96.92%), with all 37 failures concentrated in the 12% central-crop condition. Recovery increased from 829/1,200 (69.08%) at RS0 to 1,163/1,200 at RS128, while mean transmitted images per session increased from 970 to 2,676.7. At RS128, 8-, 32-, and 64-byte payloads required 2,320, 2,640, and 3,070 images, respectively. Mean partition-level macro-AUC values were 0.504, 0.513, and 0.527 for SRM-lite, GLCM, and CNN detectors; the corresponding five-partition 95% intervals were 0.502--0.506, 0.512--0.514, and 0.523--0.530. These results establish a reproducible finite-index authenticated signaling construction and quantify the principal tradeoffs among robustness, communication cost, index feasibility, and selection discrimination under the evaluated image-channel conditions.
